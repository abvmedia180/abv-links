Adult ADHD evaluation in New York: what happens and what it costs with insurance

Updated October 16, 2026

Draft pending clinician review. Not published, not indexed and not listed on the blog until Dr. Joseph approves it.

An adult ADHD evaluation in New York usually starts with a short screening call, then a full psychiatric visit of about an hour that covers your current symptoms, your childhood history and anything that can look like ADHD, such as anxiety, depression or burnout. With an in-network commercial plan, you typically pay your plan’s normal copay, coinsurance or deductible rate.

Below is how the process works at Joseph Psychiatry, and what to ask any practice before you book.

Who should think about an ADHD evaluation as an adult?

If you have always felt “smart but scattered,” it may be worth a conversation. Many adults, especially women, are first evaluated in their 20s, 30s or later. Often the trigger is a season that asks more of you than your old systems can handle: law school, residency, a demanding new job, a promotion or a new baby.

Common signs people describe:

  • Starting tasks feels impossible, even ones that matter
  • Deadlines sneak up no matter how carefully you plan
  • You lose track of details, keys and appointments
  • You work twice as hard as everyone else to stay organized, and you are exhausted

None of these alone means you have ADHD. That is exactly what an evaluation is for.

What happens during an adult ADHD evaluation?

At our practice, the steps look like this:

  1. A free 10-minute phone call. Your free consult is a quick 10-minute phone call, not a visit. We’ll see if we’re the right fit and answer your questions.
  2. A 60-minute intake by video. One of our board-certified psychiatric nurse practitioners or Dr. Joseph talks through your symptoms, how long they have been there, how things looked when you were younger, your sleep, mood and stress, and what has or has not helped before.
  3. A plan. Your clinician explains what they found and what they recommend next. Sometimes that includes formal testing. Sometimes it includes treatment options, which may or may not include medication.

Because ADHD overlaps with anxiety, depression, sleep problems and burnout, a careful evaluation takes time to tell them apart. Many people have more than one going on at once.

Do I need formal ADHD testing?

Not always. A psychiatric evaluation is a clinical assessment of your history and symptoms. Formal neuropsychological testing is a separate, longer set of tests done by a psychologist or neuropsychologist.

When formal testing would help, we refer you to trusted providers and then manage your treatment based on the results. If you already have a diagnosis from years ago, bring your records and medication history to your intake so your clinician can pick up from there.

What does an adult ADHD evaluation cost with insurance?

Cost depends on your plan, not on the diagnosis. At Joseph Psychiatry:

  • Most commercial Aetna and Anthem Blue Cross Blue Shield (formerly Empire) plans: we are in-network. You pay your plan’s copay, coinsurance or deductible rate.
  • Self-pay: $300 per visit.
  • Not accepted: Medicare, Medicaid and HMO plans, or plans linked to them.

The 10-minute consult call is always free. Formal testing, if you need it, is billed separately by the testing provider, so ask them about coverage too. See our insurance and cost page for the full table.

What should I ask before booking any practice?

  • Are you in-network with my exact plan, not just my insurance company?
  • Who will I see, and will it be the same clinician each time?
  • How long is the first visit, and what happens if testing is needed?
  • Are visits by video, and do I need to be in New York State during each one?
  • What happens if I need help between visits?

How do I get started?

If this sounds familiar, start with a conversation. Read more about adult ADHD care at Joseph Psychiatry, or book your free 10-minute call. Appointments are by video, weekdays 10 AM to 7 PM ET, for adults located anywhere in New York State.
