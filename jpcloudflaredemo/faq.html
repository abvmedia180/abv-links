Getting Started

Getting started.

What happens on the free 10-minute call? +

Your free consult is a quick 10-minute phone call, not a visit. We'll see if we're the right fit and answer your questions. There is no cost and no commitment.

Who will I see? +

One of our board-certified psychiatric nurse practitioners or Dr. Joseph. Your clinician introduces themselves at your first visit and stays your clinician. We are a psychiatrist-led team: Dr. Amanda Joseph, MD, and our board-certified psychiatric nurse practitioners.

When are appointments? +

Weekdays, 10 AM to 7 PM Eastern, by video. That includes after-work slots until 7 PM. We do not offer weekend appointments.

Is everything really by video? +

Yes. We are 100% telehealth, with no office to visit. Visits happen on a secure video platform, and the free consult is a phone call. You need to be physically in New York State at the time of each visit.

Can I be seen if I am outside New York? +

Our clinicians are licensed in New York, so you need to be located in New York State during each visit. If you travel, schedule around the days you are in the state.

Insurance & Cost

Insurance and cost.

Do you take my insurance? +

We are in-network with most commercial Aetna and Anthem Blue Cross Blue Shield (formerly Empire) plans. We don't accept Medicare or Medicaid. We also don't accept HMO plans or plans linked to Medicare or Medicaid. Self-pay is $300 per visit. We'll confirm your insurance information before your first appointment.

What does a visit cost? +

With a commercial Aetna or Anthem BCBS plan, you pay your plan's copay, coinsurance or deductible rate. Self-pay is $300 per visit.

Visits & Prescriptions

Visits and prescriptions.

How often will I be seen? +

Your first month: an intake visit plus three weekly follow-ups (4 visits). After that, at least one visit a month.

How long are visits? +

The consult call is about 10 minutes by phone. Your first full visit, the intake, is about 60 minutes by video. Follow-up visits are about 30 minutes.

Do you diagnose ADHD in adults? +

Your intake includes a careful psychiatric assessment of your symptoms and history. When formal ADHD testing is needed, we refer you to trusted providers for neuropsychological testing, then manage your treatment based on the results.

Is it too late to be evaluated for ADHD as an adult? +

No. Many adults, especially women, are first evaluated in their 20s, 30s or later, often after years of working twice as hard to keep up. If the signs feel familiar, it is worth a conversation.

Can you prescribe ADHD medication? +

If medication is clinically appropriate after a full evaluation, yes, following New York State rules and evidence-based guidelines. Medication is not prescribed at the intake visit. If it is appropriate, it may be started at your first follow-up.

Is medication required? +

No. Your clinician talks through your options with you, and the plan is built around your symptoms and your preferences.

I was diagnosed years ago. Can you continue my treatment? +

Yes. Many patients come to us to continue care after a move, a provider retiring or an insurance change. Bring your medication history to your intake and we will pick up from there.

Do you offer therapy? +

We focus on psychiatric evaluation and medication management. If you have a therapist, we are glad to coordinate with them, with your permission.

Policies

Our policies.

Is there anything you do not provide? +

We do not provide emergency or crisis services, court-ordered or forensic evaluations, disability or FMLA paperwork, school, work or housing accommodation paperwork, ESA letters, or letters for legal cases. The full list is on our What to expect page.

What if I am in crisis? +

We are not an emergency service. If you are in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline), call 911 or go to your nearest emergency room.
