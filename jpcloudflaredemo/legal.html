1. HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices

THIS NOTICE DESCRIBES HOW MEDICAL INFORMATION ABOUT YOU MAY BE USED AND DISCLOSED AND HOW YOU CAN GET ACCESS TO THIS INFORMATION. PLEASE REVIEW IT CAREFULLY.

Effective date of this Notice: November 1, 2026.

1.1 Who We Are and Our Duties

This Notice applies to Joseph Psychiatry PLLC ("Practice," "we," "us" or "our"), a New York professional limited liability company that provides outpatient psychiatric care by video telehealth to adults age 18 and older who are located in New York State during their visits. It covers our medical director, the providers on our care team (including nurse practitioners), and our staff.

We are required by law to: (a) maintain the privacy and security of your protected health information ("PHI"); (b) give you this Notice of our legal duties and privacy practices; (c) follow the terms of the Notice currently in effect; and (d) notify you if a breach of your unsecured PHI occurs. These duties come from the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 ("HIPAA"), the HITECH Act and their regulations (45 C.F.R. Parts 160 and 164), and New York law. Where New York law gives you more protection than HIPAA, we follow New York law (see Section 1.5).

1.2 How We May Use and Disclose Your PHI Without Your Authorization

A. Treatment

We may use and share your PHI to provide and coordinate your care. For example, your provider may discuss your care with another provider on our care team, send a prescription to your pharmacy, check New York's prescription monitoring program before prescribing certain medications as the law requires, or share information with your primary care provider or another clinician treating you.

B. Payment

We may use and share your PHI to bill and get paid for your care. For example, we may send your health plan information about your visit, diagnosis and treatment, or request prior authorization for a medication.

C. Health Care Operations

We may use and share your PHI to run our practice, improve care and train our team. For example, we may review records to assess the quality of care, conduct training, handle audits and legal matters, or plan our services.

D. Business Associates

We work with outside vendors that perform services for us, such as our electronic health record, scheduling, telehealth, billing, payment processing, data storage and secure communication vendors. These vendors are our "business associates." Each signs a business associate agreement that requires it to protect your PHI and use it only as our agreement and the law allow. Our patient portal is provided by SimplePractice.

E. Family Members and Others Involved in Your Care

We may share PHI that is directly relevant to their involvement with a family member, friend or other person you identify who is involved in your care or in paying for it, unless you object. If you are present, we will give you the chance to agree or object first. If you are not present or cannot agree or object (for example, in an emergency), we may share information if we decide, using professional judgment, that it is in your best interest. We may also use or share PHI to notify, or help notify, a family member or other responsible person of your location, general condition or death, including as part of disaster relief efforts.

F. As Required by Law

We will use or share your PHI when federal, state or local law requires us to.

G. Public Health Activities

We may share PHI for public health purposes, such as reporting disease, injury or vital events, reporting adverse reactions to medications or problems with products, and helping with product recalls.

H. Abuse, Neglect or Domestic Violence

We may share PHI with a government authority authorized to receive such reports if we reasonably believe you are a victim of abuse, neglect or domestic violence, to the extent the law requires or permits. When the law allows, we will tell you that a report was made.

I. Health Oversight Activities

We may share PHI with health oversight agencies for activities the law authorizes, such as audits, investigations, inspections and licensure actions.

J. Judicial and Administrative Proceedings

We may share PHI in response to a court or administrative order. We may also share PHI in response to a subpoena, discovery request or other lawful process, but only if the steps the law requires have been taken, such as notice to you or a protective order. New York law places extra limits on releasing mental health information in legal proceedings, and we follow those limits.

K. Law Enforcement

We may share PHI with law enforcement officials for purposes the law allows, such as in response to a court order, warrant or summons, to identify or locate a suspect, fugitive, material witness or missing person, about a crime victim in limited cases, or to report a crime on our premises.

L. To Avert a Serious Threat to Health or Safety

We may use or share PHI when we believe in good faith that it is necessary to prevent or lessen a serious and imminent threat to the health or safety of a person or the public, and only with someone reasonably able to prevent or lessen the threat. New York law may also require or permit mental health professionals to warn or report in these situations, including reports under New York Mental Hygiene Law Section 9.46 when a patient is likely to engage in conduct that would result in serious harm to self or others.

M. Workers' Compensation

We may share PHI as authorized by, and to the extent necessary to comply with, workers' compensation laws and similar programs.

N. Specialized Government Functions

We may share PHI for military and veterans' activities if you are a member of the armed forces, for national security and intelligence activities authorized by law, and for protective services for the President and others, as the law allows.

O. Correctional Institutions and Custody

If you are an inmate of a correctional institution or in the custody of a law enforcement official, we may share PHI with the institution or official when necessary for your health care, for the health and safety of others, or for the safety and security of the institution.

P. Coroners, Medical Examiners and Funeral Directors

We may share PHI with a coroner or medical examiner to identify a deceased person or determine a cause of death, and with funeral directors as needed for their duties.

Q. Organ and Tissue Donation

We may share PHI with organizations that handle organ, eye or tissue donation and transplantation.

R. Research

We may use or share PHI for research only when an Institutional Review Board or privacy board has approved a waiver of authorization, or as otherwise permitted by the HIPAA research rules. We do not currently conduct research.

S. Appointment Reminders and Health-Related Services

We may contact you by phone, email or the patient portal to remind you of appointments or to tell you about treatment options and health-related services that may interest you.

T. Fundraising

We do not conduct fundraising. If we ever did, you would have the right to opt out of fundraising communications.

1.3 Uses and Disclosures Requiring Your Written Authorization

We will not do the following without your written authorization: (a) use or share psychotherapy notes, except in the limited cases the law allows; (b) use or share your PHI for marketing; (c) sell your PHI; or (d) make any other use or disclosure not described in this Notice. New York law also requires your specific written consent before we share certain information, as described in Section 1.5.

You may revoke an authorization at any time by writing to our Privacy Officer. A revocation does not undo any use or disclosure we already made in reliance on the authorization.

1.4 Substance Use Disorder Records (42 C.F.R. Part 2)

We are not a federally assisted substance use disorder treatment program. If we receive records about you from a program covered by 42 C.F.R. Part 2, those records have special protection. We will not use or share those records, or testimony that relays their content, in any civil, criminal, administrative or legislative proceeding against you unless you give specific written consent, or a court issues an order that meets the requirements of Part 2 after notice and an opportunity to be heard as Part 2 requires. A subpoena alone is not enough. Part 2 records we receive for treatment, payment or health care operations may otherwise be used and shared as described in this Notice, as Part 2 and HIPAA allow. We do not use Part 2 records for fundraising.

1.5 New York State Protections

Mental health information. New York law gives mental health information extra protection. Where New York Mental Hygiene Law Section 33.13 applies, and under New York's professional confidentiality and privilege laws, we share clinical information only with your consent or as those laws specifically allow.

HIV-related information. Under New York Public Health Law Article 27-F, we will not share confidential HIV-related information without your specific written authorization on a form that meets New York requirements, except as that law permits.

Other sensitive information. Where New York or federal law gives more protection to other kinds of information, such as genetic test results, we follow the more protective law.

Access under New York law. New York Public Health Law Section 18 also gives you the right to inspect and get copies of your records, as described in Section 1.6.

Adults only. We treat only adults age 18 and older, so the rules for minors' records do not apply to our practice. A person with legal authority to act for you, such as a health care agent or court-appointed guardian, may exercise your rights as the law allows.

1.6 Your Rights

A. Right to Inspect and Get a Copy of Your Records

You may ask to see or get a copy of your PHI in our records, on paper or electronically in the form and format you request if we can readily produce it. Send your request in writing to our Privacy Officer. We will respond within 30 days, and under New York Public Health Law Section 18 we will give you an opportunity to inspect your records within 10 days of your written request. We may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee for copies that does not exceed what the law allows (for paper copies, no more than 75 cents per page under New York law). We will not deny you copies because you cannot pay. In limited cases the law allows, we may deny part of a request, for example where a provider determines that access could reasonably be expected to cause substantial and identifiable harm. If we do, we will explain why in writing and tell you how to ask for a review.

B. Right to Send an Electronic Copy to Someone Else

You may ask us in writing, signed by you, to send an electronic copy of your PHI directly to a person or organization you clearly name.

C. Right to Request an Amendment

If you believe information in your record is wrong or incomplete, you may ask us in writing to correct it. We will respond within 60 days. We may deny the request in some cases, for example if we did not create the information or it is accurate and complete. If we deny it, you may submit a statement of disagreement to be added to your record.

D. Right to an Accounting of Disclosures

You may ask for a list of certain disclosures we made of your PHI during the six years before your request. The list will not include disclosures for treatment, payment or health care operations, disclosures you authorized, and certain other disclosures. The first list in any 12-month period is free; we may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee for more.

E. Right to Request Restrictions

You may ask us to limit how we use or share your PHI for treatment, payment or health care operations. We are not required to agree, except in one case: if you pay for a service or item out of pocket in full, you may ask us not to share information about it with your health plan for payment or health care operations, and we will agree unless the law requires the disclosure.

F. Right to Request Confidential Communications

You may ask us to contact you in a certain way or at a certain address or phone number, for example only by email or only at a particular number. We will accommodate reasonable requests and will not ask why.

G. Right to a Paper Copy of This Notice

You may ask for a paper copy of this Notice at any time, even if you agreed to receive it electronically. This Notice is posted on our website, and we will send a copy by mail or email on request.

H. Right to Be Notified of a Breach

You have the right to be notified if a breach of your unsecured PHI occurs.

I. Right to Choose Someone to Act for You

If you have given someone health care power of attorney or have a legal guardian, that person may exercise your rights. We will confirm the person has this authority before we act.

1.7 Changes to This Notice

We may change this Notice, and the change will apply to all PHI we have about you, including information we created or received before the change. If we make a material change, we will post the new Notice on our website with its effective date and send you a copy on request.

1.8 Complaints

4. Telehealth Consent Disclosure

This is a summary of what you agree to when you receive care from us. You will review and sign our full telehealth consent during intake.

4.1 How Visits Work

Joseph Psychiatry PLLC provides care only by telehealth, using secure live video. We have no office open to the public and do not offer in-person or on-site visits. You and your provider will not be in the same place. At the start of each visit we may confirm your identity and your physical location.

4.2 Who We Serve and What We Treat

We provide psychiatric evaluation and medication management for adults age 18 and older who are located in New York State during each visit, for conditions including adult ADHD, anxiety and depression. Your care is provided by our medical director, Dr. Amanda Joseph, a board-certified psychiatrist, or by another provider on her care team, including nurse practitioners.

4.3 Services We Do Not Provide

  • Care for anyone under age 18.
  • In-person, on-site, nursing home or long-term care facility visits.
  • Emergency or crisis services.
  • Forensic, court-ordered or custody evaluations, or court testimony.
  • Disability, FMLA, or school, work or housing accommodation paperwork, or Emotional Support Animal letters.
  • Ketamine or related treatments.
  • Couples, marital or family therapy.
  • Medication treatment for opioid use disorder, or treatment of substance use disorders that need a higher level of care.
  • Hormone therapy.

This list is not complete. Your provider decides what care is clinically appropriate and may refer you elsewhere when another setting would serve you better. Your records are always available to you on request.

4.4 Medications and Controlled Substances

Medication decisions are made by your provider using clinical judgment, and we do not guarantee that any particular medication will be prescribed. No medication is prescribed until your evaluation is complete. Controlled substances, such as stimulants, are considered only after a full evaluation and only when clinically appropriate, in compliance with federal and New York law, including DEA rules on telemedicine prescribing and New York's prescription monitoring program. Patients taking controlled substances or starting new medications must attend follow-up visits as scheduled, and missing required follow-ups may mean a prescription cannot be continued.

4.5 Benefits, Risks and Limits of Telehealth

Telehealth lets you receive care without traveling. It also has limits:

  • A provider cannot do a physical exam by video, and video or audio quality may limit the visit.
  • Technical problems can delay or interrupt care.
  • Despite strong safeguards, electronic communication carries a small risk of a privacy breach.
  • Some conditions are better treated in person, and your provider may recommend in-person care elsewhere.

4.6 Your Responsibilities

  • Join each visit from a private place in New York State, using a device with a working camera and microphone.
  • Give us a phone number where we can reach you. If the video connection fails, we will try to reconnect or call you.
  • Tell us your physical location at each visit, so we can send help in an emergency.

4.7 Recording

We do not record visits without your written consent. Please do not record a visit, by video, audio or screenshot, without the written consent of your provider and the Practice.

4.8 Your Right to Decline

Telehealth is voluntary. You may decline telehealth or withdraw your consent at any time, and doing so will not affect your right to care elsewhere. Because we provide care only by telehealth, if you decline we will help you find other options for care.

4.9 Payment and Fees

  • Insurance: we are in network with most commercial Aetna and Anthem Blue Cross Blue Shield plans. Coverage is set by your plan, and you are responsible for any copay, coinsurance or deductible it assigns.
  • Medicare and Medicaid: we do not accept Medicare or Medicaid, or plans linked to them. If you have this coverage, please tell us before booking.
  • Self-pay: $300 per visit.
  • Late cancellation and no-show: a $200 fee applies if you cancel with less than 24 hours' notice or miss your appointment.
  • Billing: billing and payments are handled through our electronic health record and billing vendors, who sign business associate agreements with us.
  • Good Faith Estimate: under the No Surprises Act, if you are uninsured or not using insurance, you have the right to receive a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges. Ask us for one when you schedule. If your bill is $400 or more above your Good Faith Estimate, you may be able to dispute it. Learn more at www.cms.gov/nosurprises.
